## Setting (stated with every result)

- **Network:** an ENWL1 four-wire LV benchmark (UK, 800 kVA, 200 single-phase customers) populated with Flemish (Fluvius 2024) smart-meter profiles. It is not an observed Flemish feeder.
- **Population:** household technology placement seed 101 is the headline (W1 adds 4 more placements); technology mix 30% HP / 30% EV / 40% PV.
- **Limits:** UK statutory voltage limits, 216.2–253.0 V.
- **Envelope:** uniform per household (except W3); optional and incremental from zero, on top of the passive forecast; 200 design scenarios, ε = β = 0.05 (acceptance ≥ 98% of design scenarios).
- **What "verified" means.** An issued envelope passed the exact four-wire OpenDSS check of the frozen finite design-sample acceptance rule. That is **not** unconditional or 95%-confidence operational safety.
  - On fresh draws, non-zero exact (B) envelopes reach 97.4% mean four-wire feasibility, and 93.9% of them meet 0.95 empirically.
  - Only **47.3%** pass a one-sided 95% lower-bound test on 200 draws.
- **Feasibility is direction-specific and per interval.** Export envelopes are checked for overvoltage, cable and transformer loading; import envelopes for undervoltage, cable and transformer loading. There is no simultaneous guarantee over a day or year.
- **Status of the evidence.** B and C were pre-registered. Everything else (C+, W1–W3, Option 2, envelope prediction, the four-wire extension) is post-hoc or exploratory, but frozen before results were seen.

## Claims we make

1. **Dynamic envelopes recover optional capacity that a single static limit cannot.**
   - Exact four-wire-verified envelopes over 36 held-out test days give **4.81 MWh export** (season-stratified day-bootstrap interval 4.35–5.30) and **2.50 MWh import** (2.11–2.87) of envelope-hours per household per year.
   - No positive uniform year-round allowance could be verified across the sampled intervals.
   - These are envelope-hours, not delivered energy.
2. **A balanced approximation is badly unsafe on this four-wire feeder.** Balanced-model envelopes are about 2–3× larger and hold in only 2% (export) and 24% (import) of four-wire draws on the summer day.
3. **The result is robust to household placement, and the headline is conservative** (W1, 5 seeds): export 4.81–6.18, import 2.50–3.22 MWh per household per year. Seed 101 is the lowest of the five.
4. **Single-phase HP/EV connection drives much of the congestion** (W2, idealised sensitivity). With idealised three-phase connection of HP/EV homes, capacity rises about 2–2.5×, and baseline-infeasible intervals fall from about 45% to about 10%.
5. **Location- and phase-aware (spatial) envelopes release more import capacity at a cost** (W3).
   - Import rises 2.14× and export 11% under the same joint design-sample rule.
   - The trade-offs: lower held-out import robustness (85.8% vs about 93.5% meeting 0.95), 11–12× the exact calls, and up to 41% of customer-intervals worse off than under uniform.
6. **A balanced GENCO screen can triage four-wire simulations safely only with an exact gate.**
   - C reproduced 1,727 of 1,728 exact decisions with 14.65% fewer exact calls, with one material false acceptance.
   - C+ (final exact gate) matches B on 100% of intervals with 6% fewer exact calls.
   - Classical screens (exact balanced sweep, fixed point) do equally well, so GENCO adds no screening value here.
7. **Learned envelope proposals plus one exact gate cut exact OpenDSS calls** (envpred3, needs no GENCO).
   - About 79% of exact envelope-hours retained with about 6.7% of the exact calls, or about 90% with about 21%.
   - Fresh four-wire feasibility of issued envelopes is 0.991.
   - This is an **exact-call reduction**, not a measured wall-clock speed-up.
8. **GENCO needs adaptation for LV, and the failure modes are explained.**
   - The stock graphkit edge encoder cannot see impedance magnitude (its final LayerNorm cancels |Y|).
   - The physics loss collapses, and voltage-difference flows are ill-conditioned.
   - With asinh admittance features, a linear prior and KCL flows, Vm MAE is 1.0e-3 pu, about 5× better than the prior on envelope rows.
   - Two tooling bugs were found: graphkit's best-model export and datakit's 1.5 pu slack.
9. **A GENCO-style phase-aware GNN can correct a cheap four-wire prior** (exploratory four-wire extension, one feeder).
   - Against a one-iteration four-wire sweep (U1): V_pn MAE −91%, OpenDSS fallbacks 35.8% → 5.8%, near-limit balanced accuracy 0.926 → 0.992.
   - The exact four-wire gate preserved the frozen acceptance rule and produced **zero observed false-safe decisions** on the held-out evaluation.

## Claims we do not make

- **That GENCO adds value over specialised classical methods on this problem.** Every GENCO-specific test is negative or neutral:
  - balanced screen ≈ classical screens;
  - Option 2 GENCO arm worse than phase features;
  - envpred3 PCG − P = −0.06 points;
  - PhaseGENCO − PhaseGraph = **+6.0 points more** fallbacks.
- **That phase-aware representation causally explains the four-wire result.** U1 is already phase-aware. The comparison shows the value of the learned residual. Balanced baselines support, but do not isolate, the role of phase information.
- **That the four-wire GNN beats classical physics.** The iterated four-wire sweep is about 80× more accurate (0.1% fallback). End-to-end times (2.1 vs 5.5 ms per state) mix an A100 model with an unoptimised CPU sweep and are not a speed claim.
- **"Certified" envelopes, unconditional or statutory four-wire safety, or safety from zero flexibility.** Passive baselines are often infeasible, especially on winter evenings.
- **Delivered EV/PV energy, curtailment avoided, customer utilisation, reinforcement deferral or economic value.**
- **Generalisation** to a real Flemish feeder, other feeders, other technology mixes or other years.
  - Intervals cover day-to-day variation within the sampled days only.
  - Test dates were also used during development, so post-hoc results are exploratory.
- **That GENCO is faster than exact solvers.** Specialised Z-bus and sweep solvers are comparable or faster on this 398-bus feeder.
